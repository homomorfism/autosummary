# E3 — Quiz question psychometrics on production attempts

**Code:** `app/quality.py` (item analysis, rejection rule, LLM regeneration) ·
**Data:** `data/quiz_results.prod.db` (attempts recorded by the `web/` quiz app
in production, 2026-07-16 → 2026-07-18) · **Run:**
`uv run python -m app.quality data/<course>/<lecture> --db data/quiz_results.prod.db`

## Goal

Measure whether the LLM-generated quizzes (`app/quiz.py`, 5 single-choice
questions × 4 options per lecture) actually work as measurement instruments on
real respondents: per-item **difficulty** (p-value = share of correct answers)
and **discrimination** (point-biserial correlation r_pb between item
correctness and the *rest score*, i.e. the attempt score excluding the item
itself). The rejection rule (p ∈ [0.30, 0.85], r_pb ≥ 0.15, from 5 attempts)
then decides which questions survive and which get retired and regenerated
(the closed loop of `--retire` / `--regenerate`).

## Data

The deployed course site collected **49 attempts** from **8 distinct
respondents** (anonymous `quiz_client_id` cookies; one attempt per browser per
quiz) over all **9 lectures** of `deep-learning-and-computer-vision` — 5–6
attempts per quiz, so every one of the **45 items** clears the
`--min-attempts 5` threshold. Three respondents completed all 9 quizzes.

Attempt scores span the full 0–5 range (mean 3.06/5, SD 1.54) — real variance
in respondent ability, which is what point-biserial needs to be meaningful at
all.

## Results

**33 / 45 items (73 %) pass the rejection rule.** Aggregate difficulty is well
placed: mean p = 0.61 (target window midpoint ≈ 0.58), 39/45 items inside the
p-window. Discrimination is the weaker axis: mean r_pb = +0.42, but 7 items
have *negative* r_pb (respondents who got them right scored *lower* on the
rest of the quiz).

### The 12 flagged items, by failure mode

**Too easy (2)** — p = 1.00, all 5 respondents correct, r_pb undefined (no
variance). Both are "the one fact everyone remembers" questions: why
nonlinearities are essential (lecture-02) and how ResNet skip connections help
gradients (lecture-05). Zero measurement value; prime candidates for
regeneration into a subtler probe of the same topic.

**Too hard (4)** — p = 0.20 (1 of 5 correct). These split into two distinct
sub-cases:

- *Hard but discriminating* — the two lecture-07 items (DETR avoiding NMS,
  deformable-attention complexity) have **r_pb = +0.71**: the one respondent
  who got each right is the strongest respondent. In both, 3 of 5 respondents
  converged on the *same* distractor — either a genuinely attractive
  misconception or a debatable answer key. Worth a manual key review before
  mechanically regenerating.
- *Hard and noisy* — lecture-01's history-of-AI question (r_pb ≈ 0) and
  lecture-02's gradient-descent-for-logistic-regression question
  (r_pb = −0.13, again 3/5 on one distractor). These fail on both axes.

**Low discrimination (6)** — all six sit at moderate-to-high p (0.40–0.83) with
r_pb between −0.69 and −0.08. The dominant pattern at this sample size: 4-of-5
answer correctly and the single miss comes from a *strong* respondent, which
flips r_pb hard negative (e.g. ML-vs-MAP in lecture-02 at −0.69). Lecture-03 is
the worst quiz on this axis — 3 of its 5 items don't separate strong from weak
respondents (SGD with constant learning rate, ReLU properties, forward-mode
autodiff).

## Interpretation

- **The generation pipeline mostly lands in the target difficulty band.** The
  anti-length-leak validators in `app/quiz.py` plus "test fundamentals, not
  trivia" prompting produce items averaging p ≈ 0.6 — quiz-appropriate without
  any difficulty calibration signal at generation time.
- **Discrimination is where LLM generation fails silently.** All 12 rejected
  items *look* fine as text; only respondent data reveals that some don't
  correlate with understanding. This is the argument for the closed loop:
  no static validator could have flagged them.
- **Later lectures produced better quizzes.** Lectures 07–09 have the highest
  mean r_pb (+0.64…+0.66) and the lowest scores — harder material spreads
  respondents out, which is exactly when item statistics become informative.

## Closed loop

This run is **report-only**: `quiz.json` files are unchanged and the
`retired_questions` table in the prod DB is empty. Applying the loop:

```bash
# retire the 12 flagged items (archived with stats into the DB)
uv run python -m app.quality data/deep-learning-and-computer-vision/lecture-0{1,2,3,5,6,7} \
    --db data/quiz_results.prod.db --retire
# …or retire + generate targeted LLM replacements (prompt carries p, r_pb and
# the miss direction: too easy → subtler; too hard → more direct; low r_pb → less guessable)
uv run python -m app.quality data/deep-learning-and-computer-vision/lecture-0{1,2,3,5,6,7} \
    --db data/quiz_results.prod.db --regenerate
```

Regenerated questions get fresh content-hash `question_id`s, so respondents who
already submitted can re-attempt once a quiz no longer shares any question with
their recorded attempt (the web app's resubmission rule).

## Caveats

- **n = 5–6 per item is the floor, not a comfortable sample.** p has a
  granularity of 0.17–0.20 and a single respondent swings r_pb by ±0.5 or more
  — the negative-r_pb flags are mostly "one strong respondent missed an easy
  item". The thresholds are deliberately soft and the point is the
  *methodology* (measure → reject → regenerate), not the significance of any
  single item verdict.
- **Respondents are not a class.** 8 self-selected people, three of whom took
  all 9 quizzes in one or two sittings; later-lecture scores may partly reflect
  fatigue rather than difficulty.
- **The rejection rule conflates two kinds of "too hard".** The lecture-07
  pair shows p < 0.30 can coexist with excellent discrimination; a future
  refinement could spare high-r_pb hard items (or route them to a "hard mode")
  instead of retiring them.
- Item statistics assume the answer key is correct. Where a majority converges
  on one distractor, key review should precede regeneration.
